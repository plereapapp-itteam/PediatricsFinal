🗣️Development, Speech & ADHD

  • Speech-language referral thresholds: <3 words at 15 mo, <25 words at 24 mo, no unique 2-word combinations by 30 mo.
  • First test for speech delay: audiologic assessment + speech-language/developmental evaluation. Routine EEG is not first-line unless seizures or language regression suggest an epileptic disorder.
  • GDD: delay in ≥2 developmental domains. Regression at any age is a RED FLAG, never a normal variant.
  • Autism screening is classically done at 18 and 24 months; a positive screen → comprehensive evaluation + early intervention + audiology.

ADHD
  • Criteria: functional impairment in ≥2 settings, duration ≥6 months, onset before 12 years, ≥6 symptoms in a domain for children <17 years (≥5 from age 17).
  • First-line pharmacotherapy: methylphenidate, with response bands of roughly <0.5, 0.5–1, and 1–1.5 mg/kg/day; short-acting maximum about 60 mg/day. Worked example: 30 kg × 0.5 = 15 mg/day.
  • Alternative: atomoxetine, starting ~0.3 mg/kg/day and titrating toward 1.2–1.8 mg/kg/day. Behavioral parent/teacher therapy is a core adjunct, especially in younger children.
  • Before a stimulant: check height, weight, HR, BP, cardiac symptoms and family history. Urgent referral for syncope, arrhythmia, significant cardiac disease, or severe psychiatric symptoms.
Board trapHyperactivity only at home is not ADHD. One setting, or a duration under 6 months, fails the criteria no matter how severe the behavior sounds.

⚠️Chemotherapy Safety Rule

Dosing rulePediatric antineoplastic treatment is protocol- and BSA-based (mg/m²). Do not invent an mg/kg equivalent when the source gives none. Calculate BSA when the protocol supplies mg/m²: √[(height cm × weight kg)/3600] — 120 cm and 25 kg gives 0.91 m².

👁️Early Childhood Tumors — Under 5 Years

Retinoblastoma
Classic age <3 yr · RB1, 13q14
  • Normal: symmetric red reflex. RED FLAG: leukocoria (most common initial sign), then strabismus, then proptosis.
  • Diagnosis: indirect ophthalmoscopy / examination under anesthesia; MRI for optic-nerve or intracranial extension. Hereditary disease is earlier, bilateral and multifocal.
  • Treatment: small tumors → focal laser or cryotherapy; larger bilateral disease → chemoreduction + focal therapy; enucleation for an advanced unilateral eye with unsalvageable vision.
Do notNever biopsy the eye. Leukocoria goes straight to urgent ophthalmology; proptosis, orbital extension, or neurologic signs escalate to emergency ophthalmology/oncology.
Neuroblastoma
Infant/toddler · adrenal or sympathetic-chain abdominal mass
  • Marker: urinary VMA/HVA elevated in ~95%. Imaging: US/CT/MRI plus MIBG.
  • Metastatic clue: periorbital ecchymosis/proptosis. Neurologic clue: opsoclonus-myoclonus-ataxia. Autonomic clues: Horner syndrome, sweating, hypertension.
  • Treatment: low-risk localized → surgery; favorable infant stage 4S/MS may be observed if asymptomatic; high-risk → intensive multiagent therapy + surgery ± transplant/targeted therapy.
  • EMERGENCY: spinal-cord compression (urgent MRI and treatment to prevent permanent injury), massive hepatomegaly with respiratory compromise, tumor lysis syndrome.
Wilms Tumor
Classic age 2–5 yr · rare under 6 months
  • Vignette: painless unilateral abdominal/flank mass, sometimes with hypertension (about 25%), painless hematuria, or fever. Syndromes: WAGR, Beckwith-Wiedemann/hemihyperplasia.
  • First test: renal ultrasound with Doppler; CT abdomen/chest for staging.
  • Treatment: radical nephrectomy + lymph-node sampling; stage I–II favorable → vincristine + dactinomycin; stage III–IV → add doxorubicin ± radiotherapy.
Do notRoutine pre-operative biopsy can upstage the disease in a classic COG presentation, and repeated aggressive palpation risks rupture. Upfront surgery is the favored approach.

👶Dysmorphology & Chromosomal Disorders

  • Normal variant: a single isolated minor anomaly with an otherwise normal examination, or a familial facial feature without functional abnormality.
  • RED FLAG: ≥3 minor anomalies (which raises the probability of an associated major malformation to roughly 20–30%), ≥2 major anomalies, growth or developmental delay with anomalies, regression, microcephaly, seizures, CHD, renal defect, or ambiguous genitalia.
  • First-line test for multiple congenital anomalies or intellectual disability: chromosomal microarray / array CGH. Use karyotype when a whole-chromosome aneuploidy or a balanced rearrangement is suspected.
  • No drug corrects the chromosome abnormality — treat the specific cardiac, renal, endocrine, metabolic or developmental problem and start early intervention.

🗺️30-Second Age Map

  • Newborn: chromosomal syndrome, dysmorphic syndrome, IEM, congenital neuroblastoma.
  • <2 yr: retinoblastoma, neuroblastoma.
  • 2–5 yr: ALL, Wilms tumor.
  • >5 yr: NHL increasingly likely.
  • Adolescent: Hodgkin lymphoma, Ewing sarcoma, AML relatively more common.

🧩Pattern Pairings

  • Petechiae = platelet or vessel problem; hemarthrosis = coagulation-factor defect.
  • Microcytic + low ferritin → iron deficiency. Microcytic + disproportionately low MCV + preserved RBC count → thalassemia.
  • Petechiae + isolated low platelets + well child after a viral illness → ITP. Petechiae + toxic child + ↑PT + ↑aPTT + ↓fibrinogen → DIC.
  • Hemarthrosis + normal platelets/PT + ↑aPTT → hemophilia A or B. Epistaxis/menorrhagia with usually normal platelets → VWD.
  • Boy + recurrent pyogenic infection after 6–9 months + absent B cells → XLA. Older child + recurrent sinopulmonary infection + low Ig + B cells present → CVID.
  • Transient hypogammaglobulinemia has B cells present and eventually recovers; XLA has B cells absent and is permanent.
  • Hives only → H1 antihistamine. Hives + airway or circulatory compromise → IM epinephrine.

🚨DKA — Must-Memorize Sequence

  1. Assess ABCs, perfusion, neurologic status, glucose, ketones, electrolytes, venous gas.
  2. Controlled isotonic fluid replacement.
  3. Start IV insulin after initial fluid therapy — no routine insulin bolus.
  4. Regular insulin 0.05–0.1 U/kg/hr. Worked: 25 kg × 0.05 = 1.25 U/hr.
  5. Add glucose-containing IV fluid as serum glucose falls while ketosis and acidosis persist.

🆘Adrenal Crisis & Diabetes Insipidus

Adrenal crisis
  • Vomiting + weakness + hypotension/shock → treat immediately, do not wait for confirmation.
  • IV hydrocortisone — source bolus: 10 mg infant, 25 mg toddler, 50 mg older child, 100 mg adolescent.
  • Give glucose-containing IV therapy for hypoglycemia and isotonic saline for hyponatremia/hypovolemia; treat severe hyperkalemia urgently. Avoid hypotonic fluid during the acute crisis.
Board trapWith combined hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency, give the glucocorticoid first. Starting levothyroxine before replacing cortisol can precipitate a crisis.
Diabetes insipidus cutpoints
  • Polyuria/polydipsia >2 L/m²/day is the pathologic threshold.
  • Serum osm >300 + urine osm <300 → DI established.
  • Serum osm <270, or urine osm >600, makes DI unlikely; osm 270–300 with symptoms → consider supervised water-deprivation testing.

  • Escalation: repeat IVIG and/or steroid-based rescue in resistant disease. RED FLAG: shock, myocarditis, persistent fever after IVIG, coronary thrombosis.
Board trapDo not exclude Kawasaki because only three classic features are present — incomplete KD is real, especially in an infant <6 months with prolonged unexplained fever, and echo or laboratory findings can support it. Antibiotics alone is the wrong answer.
Acute Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease
Typical age 5–15 yr, weeks after GAS pharyngitis

🫀Older Child, Endocarditis & Arrest

  • ASD: fixed wide split S₂ with a pulmonary flow murmur. Closure is indicated for symptoms or Qp:Qs ≥2:1 or RV enlargement — transcatheter if suitable, otherwise surgery. A small ASD without RV enlargement may be observed; do not treat a significant ASD indefinitely with diuretics.

AVOID routine shunt closure once irreversible physiology is established, and avoid routine phlebotomy — assess iron deficiency and hyperviscosity first. Prevention is timely repair before irreversible pulmonary vascular disease.
  • Pediatric hypertension: under 13 years use age/sex/height percentiles; from 13 yearsSupp elevated is 120–129/<80, stage 1 is 130/80–139/89, stage 2 is ≥140/90. Respiratory 10%

👶Infant & Toddler

Bronchiolitis
  • Classic age <2 yr, especially infants: viral prodrome → cough + wheeze/crackles + tachypnea.
  • FIRST-LINE: supportive — suction + hydration + oxygen if hypoxemic. There is no routine drug.
  • AVOID routinely: albuterol, epinephrine, steroids, antibiotics.
  • RED FLAG: infant <6–12 weeks, apnea, cyanosis, severe retractions, dehydration, poor feeding, exhaustion, worsening oxygen requirement. If too tachypneic to feed safely, use NG or IV support rather than forcing oral intake. Worked fluid: 8 kg → 800 mL/day maintenance.
  • Board distinction: bronchiolitis is infant + diffuse small-airway disease; focal crackles with high fever suggests pneumonia.
Croup
  • Classic age 6 mo–3 yr; hallmark is barking cough + hoarseness + inspiratory stridor.
  • FIRST-LINE: dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg once. Worked: 12 kg → 7.2 mg. Alternative nebulized budesonide 2 mg; moderate to severe obstruction adds nebulized epinephrine.
  • AVOID: humidified air as definitive therapy — it has not demonstrated benefit.
  • EMERGENCY: stridor at rest with cyanosis, exhaustion or poor air entry → keep the child calm, oxygen, steroid, nebulized epinephrine, airway team.
  • RED FLAG for another diagnosis: drooling + tripod position + toxic appearance → epiglottitis or deep-neck disease.

⚖️Acid–Base & Air Leaks

Respiratory acidosis
↓pH + ↑PaCO₂
Causes: severe bronchiolitis, pneumonia or asthma, CNS depression, neuromuscular weakness. Acute compensation HCO₃⁻ +1 per 10 mmHg rise; chronic +3.5–4 per 10. Worked: pH 7.25 with PaCO₂ 60 → expected acute HCO₃⁻ ≈ 26 mEq/L. FIRST-LINE: improve ventilation and treat the cause — avoid routine bicarbonate.
Respiratory alkalosis
↑pH + ↓PaCO₂
Causes: hypoxemia, early sepsis or pneumonia, pain, fever, excessive ventilation. Acute compensation HCO₃⁻ −2 per 10 mmHg fall. Worked: PaCO₂ 20 → expected HCO₃⁻ ≈ 20 mEq/L. Treat the cause; never diagnose "anxiety" before excluding hypoxemia, sepsis or toxin, and avoid paper-bag rebreathing in a hypoxemic child.
Pneumothorax
Unilateral ↓breath sounds + HYPERRESONANCE
Stable disease → CXR or lung ultrasound. Tension pneumothorax = distress + unilateral absent sounds + shock → immediate needle or finger decompression, then chest tube. No mg/kg drug corrects it, and do not wait for a CXR when tension physiology is clinically evident. Sudden collapse on positive-pressure ventilation is the classic red flag.
Pneumomediastinum
Chest pain + subcutaneous crepitus · Hamman crunchSupp
Common triggers: cough, asthma, Valsalva. Stable disease → rest, analgesia, treat the trigger; routine invasive drainage and routine surgery are wrong answers. RED FLAG: forceful vomiting + severe chest pain or toxicity → evaluate for esophageal perforation; shock → search for pneumothorax, airway or esophageal injury.

🎯Age Pairings That Carry the Most Items

Newborn and infancy
  • Newborn + bilious vomit → obstruction / malrotation-volvulus.
  • 3–6-week infant + projectile nonbilious vomit + hungry afterward → pyloric stenosis.
  • Delayed meconium + distention + empty rectum → Hirschsprung.
  • Jaundice <24 h, or jaundice + pale stool / dark urine → pathologic; fractionate the bilirubin.
  • Normal at birth → vomiting/lethargy/seizure after feeds → inborn error of metabolism.
  • Newborn + snuffles + palms/soles rash → congenital syphilis. + petechiae + microcephaly + hearing loss → congenital CMV. + cataracts/PDA/deafness → congenital rubella.
  • Neonate + unexplained seizure or hepatitis, with or without vesicles → neonatal HSV → IV acyclovir now.
  • Young infant + apnea without a whoop → pertussis.
  • Newborn screen abnormal but the infant looks normal → congenital hypothyroidism can still be present → levothyroxine now, scan later.
  • Infant vomiting + hypoNa + hyperK + hypoglycemia → salt-wasting CAH.
Toddler and preschool
  • 5 mo–3 yr + episodic colic + vomiting + lethargy → intussusception.
  • 2–5 yr + painless abdominal mass → Wilms; toddler + mass + periorbital ecchymoses + high VMA/HVA → neuroblastoma.
  • Leukocoria → retinoblastoma → urgent ophthalmology, never a biopsy.
  • 2–3 yr + pallor + fever + bruising + night bone pain → ALL, even with a normal WBC.
  • Postviral petechiae + isolated thrombocytopenia in a well child → ITP.
  • 6 mo–3 yr + barking cough + stridor → croup → dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg.
  • Infant <2 yr + coryza → wheeze/crackles → bronchiolitis → supportive care only.
  • Preschool child + fever ≥5 days + conjunctivitis/oral/extremity/rash findings → Kawasaki → IVIG 2 g/kg + aspirin.
  • 2-yr-old + generalized 4-minute febrile seizure + normal afterward → simple febrile seizure → no EEG, no chronic AED.
  • 1–8 yr + generalized edema + UPCR >2 + low albumin + normal C3 → MCNS → prednisone 2 mg/kg/day.
School age and adolescence
  • 5–12 yr + cola urine + HTN + edema 1–2 weeks after sore throat → APSGN with low C3.
  • Hematuria occurring WITH a URI and normal C3 → IgA nephropathy.
  • School child + migratory arthritis weeks after GAS → acute rheumatic fever → benzathine penicillin prophylaxis.
  • School child + Gottron papules + proximal weakness → JDM. Young child + ≤4 joints + ANA positive → oligo-JIA with uveitis screening.
  • Adolescent + multisystem rash/arthritis/nephritis → SLE (never ANA alone).
  • Adolescent + sudden unilateral scrotal pain + absent cremasteric reflex → torsion → theatre now.
  • Sexually active adolescent + cervical motion, uterine OR adnexal tenderness → PID — one criterion is enough.
  • Adolescent + posterior cervical nodes + splenomegaly → EBV → avoid contact sports.
  • Nighttime anal pruritus → pinworm tape test. Greasy foul stool → Giardia. Freshwater exposure + hepatosplenomegaly → S. japonicum.
  • Floodwater + calf tenderness + conjunctival suffusion → leptospirosis.
  • Fever falling but dengue warning signs appearing → critical phase, not recovery.
  • Previously acyanotic large shunt now cyanotic with clubbing → Eisenmenger → do not close it.

🚫30-Second Distractors — Each One Is a Wrong Answer

Nutrition, development and dosing
  • Complementary food at 3 months; or a 6-month-old still exclusively breastfed with no complementary food being called correct.
  • Hyperactivity in one setting, or symptoms under 6 months, diagnosed as ADHD.
  • Iron written as "ferrous sulfate mg/kg" without the elemental conversion; vitamin D for rickets multiplied by body weight.
  • Holliday-Segar used as a resuscitation bolus; jaundice at 12 hours called physiologic; hypoglycemic seizure treated only by feeding.
  • Developmental regression managed by "observe and reassess in 6 months".
Oncology, hematology and immunology
  • Normal WBC used to exclude ALL; leukemia anemia assumed to be iron deficiency; thrombocytopenic bleeding "corrected" with vitamin K.
  • Ocular biopsy for leukocoria; routine pre-op biopsy for classic Wilms; major surgical debulking as primary NHL treatment.
  • Antihistamine given instead of epinephrine in anaphylaxis; anti-D given to an Rh-negative child; routine platelet transfusion for mild ITP.
  • IVIG 0.8–1 g/kg and 400 mg/kg/month used interchangeably; live OPV given in XLA.
Endocrine, renal and GI
  • Levothyroxine started before cortisol in combined deficiency; beta blockade before alpha blockade in pheochromocytoma.
  • DKA managed with a routine insulin bolus, routine bicarbonate, or insulin stopped once glucose normalizes while ketosis persists.
  • Positive bag urine culture called a confirmed UTI; nitrofurantoin used for pyelonephritis; recurrent UTI managed by rotating antibiotics rather than looking for constipation, VUR or obstruction.
  • Nephrotic edema assumed to be hypervolemia; low C3 expected in classic MCNS; APSGN C3 expected to stay low indefinitely.
  • Calcium gluconate described as lowering potassium; severe protein restriction imposed on a child with CKD.
  • Bilious vomiting called GERD; pyloric stenosis said to be bilious; dehydrated alkalotic pyloric stenosis taken straight to theatre.
  • Waiting for currant-jelly stool; constipation from birth called functional; juice or soda equated with ORS; breastfeeding stopped during gastroenteritis.
  • Kerosene ingestion treated with lavage, emesis or charcoal; salicylate toxicity called pure metabolic acidosis; INH seizures treated with benzodiazepine alone.
Neurologic, cardiac, infectious and respiratory
  • A 3-month-old febrile seizure called simple; complex febrile seizure said to require all three criteria; routine EEG or CT after a first simple febrile seizure; antipyretics claimed to prevent recurrence.
  • Waiting 30 minutes to treat status; lorazepam 10 mg for a 20-kg child; LP performed first in shock with suspected meningitis, or with papilledema and a mass.
  • Acyclovir withheld until the HSV PCR returns; CP described as progressive; rapidly rising head circumference ignored because the infant looks comfortable.
  • Oxygen alone for TGA; fixed split S₂ attributed to VSD; the loudest murmur assumed to be the largest VSD; a softening Tet-spell murmur read as improvement; the child laid flat during a spell.
  • Eisenmenger shunt closed anyway; ARF said to need a positive throat culture; penicillin stopped once ARF symptoms resolve; IE prophylaxis given for every murmur.
  • Metronidazole alone for amebiasis; stool O&P as the best pinworm test; S. haematobium named for Philippine schistosomiasis; deworming alone for hookworm anemia.
  • Antitoxin delayed for culture in diphtheria; antibiotics before fluids in cholera; routine antibiotics for STEC or uncomplicated NTS; aggressive throat inspection in epiglottitis.
  • Infant HIV diagnosed on antibody alone; dengue platelets transfused for the number; NSAIDs given in dengue; defervescence called recovery.
  • Routine salbutamol or steroid for bronchiolitis; humidified air as definitive croup therapy; every productive cough or every pneumonia given antibiotics or a chest X-ray.
  • Dullness attributed to pneumothorax; tension pneumothorax confirmed radiographically before decompression; normal SpO₂ used to exclude CO₂ retention; bicarbonate given first for respiratory acidosis; a falling respiratory rate read as recovery.
